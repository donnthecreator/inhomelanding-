Agents

What an agent is, and when your son needs one

Since NIL opened up, parents of good high school players hear from "agents," "marketing reps" and "advisors" early. Most of it is noise. Here is the short version, and Coach AI will read any specific pitch you forward it.

  • 01An agent earns a percentage of money your son makes. A junior with no NIL deals has nothing for an agent to earn from. Anyone pushing representation on him now is selling something else.
  • 02Agents are regulated by state law. In most states an athlete agent has to be registered, and your state's high school association sets whether and how a high school athlete can do NIL at all. Check both before anyone signs anything.
  • 03A legitimate agent negotiates deals that already exist and gets paid out of them. A legitimate agent does not charge up front, does not promise offers, and does not contact college coaches for him.
  • 04If a real deal shows up, usually senior year or after he signs, that is when a conversation about representation makes sense. Division I recruits also have to report deals of $600 or more, so keep the paperwork.

Walk away if you hear

  • "Pay a fee to get started" or "a one-time setup cost"
  • "We have relationships with the coaches at..."
  • "We can get him offers"
  • "Sign now, exclusive, three years"
  • "Don't mention this to his coach"
  • Any contract before there is a deal to put in it

InHome is not an agent and does not represent athletes. We scout film and teach families how the process works. Nothing on this page is legal advice.
